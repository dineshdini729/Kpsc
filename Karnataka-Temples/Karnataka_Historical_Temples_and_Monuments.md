## 1. Architectural styles of Karnataka at a glance

| Style / phase | Period | Key features | Best examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kadamba** | 4th–6th c | Earliest Karnataka style; stepped pyramidal *shikhara* topped by a *kalasha* (the "Kadamba shikhara", later reused by Hoysalas and Vijayanagara) | Madhukeshwara (Banavasi), Pranaveshwara (Talagunda) |
| **Western Ganga** | 4th–10th c | Simple Dravida temples, Jain basadis, *manastambhas*, colossal monoliths | Gommateshwara (Shravanabelagola), Panchakuta Basadi (Kambadahalli) |
| **Early (Badami) Chalukya** | 6th–8th c | "Laboratory/cradle of Indian temple architecture"; rock-cut caves, then structural temples in **both Nagara (north) and Dravida (south)** forms side by side; red sandstone | Badami caves, Aihole, Pattadakal, Mahakuta |
| **Rashtrakuta** | 8th–10th c | Continued Dravida; rock-cut grandeur (Ellora Kailasa in Maharashtra); Jain patronage | Jain Narayana (Pattadakal), Navalinga (Kukkanur) |
| **Kalyani (Western) Chalukya** / "Gadag style" | 10th–12th c | **Vesara** (hybrid) style; soapstone (chloritic schist); ornate lathe-turned pillars, stepped (staggered square) plans, decorated doorways, stepped wells (*kalyanis*) | Mahadeva (Itagi), Lakkundi, Trikuteshwara (Gadag), Doddabasappa (Dambal) |
| **Hoysala** | 11th–14th c | Evolved Vesara; **stellate (star-shaped) plan** on a raised platform (*jagati*); soapstone; horizontal friezes (elephants, lions, horses, scrolls, epics); *madanikas*/*shilabalikas* (bracket figures); lathe-turned pillars; **sculptors signed their work** (Ruvari Mallitamma, Dasoja, Chavana, Kedaroja); *ekakuta*, *dvikuta*, *trikuta*, *chatushkuta*, *panchakuta* layouts | Belur, Halebidu, Somanathapura |
| **Vijayanagara** | 14th–17th c | Dravida; granite; huge **rayagopuras**; *kalyana mantapas*; pillars with rearing horses and *yalis*; **musical pillars**; separate *Amman* (goddess) shrines; temple chariot streets and bazaars | Hampi (Virupaksha, Vittala, Hazara Rama), Vidyashankara (Sringeri) |
| **Keladi / Ikkeri** | 16th–18th c | Blend of Hoysala, Vijayanagara and Kadamba features plus coastal elements | Aghoreshwara (Ikkeri), Rameshwara (Keladi) |
| **Mysuru Wodeyar** | 17th–20th c | Tall Dravida gopuras; Indo-Saracenic palaces | Chamundeshwari gopura, palace temples |
| **Coastal (Tulu Nadu / Kerala type)** | Medieval to living | Sloping tiled or copper roofs, wooden elements, *bhuta* (spirit) shrines, laterite | Kukke Subramanya, Udupi, Kateel, Kollur |
| **Indo-Islamic (Deccan)** | 14th–17th c | Domes, arches, minarets (Bahmani, Adil Shahi, Barid Shahi) | Gol Gumbaz, Ibrahim Rauza, Bidar Madrasa |

**Remember:** Karnataka is the only Indian state where **Nagara, Dravida and Vesara** styles stand together. Pattadakal is the classic example, with Nagara and Dravida temples in one complex.

## 3. Kadambas of Banavasi (345–540 CE)

**Political history:** Founded by **Mayurasharma**, a Brahmin who rebelled against the Pallavas of Kanchi. Capital: **Banavasi** (Uttara Kannada). **Kakusthavarma** was the greatest ruler. The Kadambas were the **first native dynasty to use Kannada in administration**. The **Talagunda pillar inscription** (Sanskrit, composed by Kubja, of Shantivarma's time) narrates Mayurasharma's story. The **Halmidi inscription** (c. 450 CE, Hassan district) is the **oldest known Kannada inscription**. The dynasty was absorbed by the Badami Chalukyas. Later branches were the **Kadambas of Hangal and Goa**.

| Temple | Place | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Madhukeshwara Temple** | Banavasi, Uttara Kannada | Original Kadamba shrine to Shiva (Madhukeshwara), rebuilt and extended by later dynasties (Chalukya, Hoysala, Vijayanagara, Sonda). Features a carved **stone cot (*kallina manche*)** gifted by the Sonda rulers (17th c). Banavasi is also mentioned in Ashokan-era Buddhist texts (Vanavasi). The **Kadambotsava** festival is held here. |
| **Pranaveshwara Temple** | Talagunda, Shivamogga | Among the oldest temples in Karnataka; the **Talagunda pillar inscription** stands here |
| **Kadamba shikhara** | — | Stepped, pyramidal tower without decoration, ending in a *kalasha*; seen later at Doddagaddavalli (Hoysala) and Hampi |

## 4. Western Gangas, Banas and Nolambas (c. 350–1000 CE)

**Political history:** The Western Gangas ruled Gangavadi (south Karnataka) from **Kolar**, then **Talakad**. **Durvinita** was a scholar-king. **Shivamara**, **Rachamalla** and **Marasimha II** were strong patrons of **Jainism**. The dynasty ended when the **Cholas captured Talakad (c. 1004)**. Their minister **Chavundaraya** built the Gommateshwara statue. The **Banas** and **Nolambas** were feudatories in the Kolar–Tumakuru–Chitradurga belt.

| Temple / monument | Place | Date / patron | Details |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Gommateshwara (Bahubali)** | Vindhyagiri hill, **Shravanabelagola**, Hassan | **c. 981–983 CE**; **Chavundaraya**, minister of Ganga king **Rachamalla IV** | **57 ft (≈17 m) monolith**, one of the largest free-standing monoliths in the world. **Mahamastakabhisheka** every 12 years: last in **2018** (88th), next due **2030**. Inscriptions in Kannada, Tamil and Marathi at its base, including one of the earliest Marathi inscriptions. Proposed for UNESCO (2026). |
| **Chandragiri hill basadis** | Shravanabelagola | 8th–12th c | **Chavundaraya Basadi**; **Bhadrabahu cave**. Tradition says **Chandragupta Maurya** came here with Jain saint Bhadrabahu and died by *sallekhana*. Over 100 inscriptions. |
| **Panchalinga temples** and **Vaidyeshwara Temple** | **Talakadu**, Mysuru (Kaveri bank) | Ganga origins; Chola and Hoysala additions | Five Shiva shrines (Vaidyeshwara, Arkeshwara, Pataleshwara, Maruleshwara, Mallikarjuna). Talakad lies under **sand dunes**, linked to the "curse of Alamelamma" on the Wodeyars. The **Panchalinga Darshana** festival is held at irregular intervals (on a Kartika new moon with a special star combination). |
| **Kirtinarayana Temple** | Talakadu | **1117, Hoysala Vishnuvardhana** | Built to celebrate the defeat of the Cholas at Talakad (1116) |
| **Panchakuta Basadi** | **Kambadahalli**, Mandya | c. 10th c, Ganga | Five-shrine Jain temple in Dravida style; tall *manastambha* (Brahmadeva pillar) |
| **Nageshwara Temple** | **Begur**, Bengaluru | Ganga, 9th–10th c | The **Begur inscription (c. 890 CE)** gives the **earliest known mention of "Bengaluru"** (records the "Bengaluru war") |
| **Bhoga Nandishwara Temple** | **Nandi village**, foot of Nandi Hills, Chikkaballapur | 9th c (Bana queen **Ratnavali**); additions by Gangas, Cholas, Hoysalas and Vijayanagara | Twin shrines (Bhoga Nandishwara and Arunachaleshwara) plus Uma-Maheshwara with an ornate Vijayanagara *kalyana mantapa*; *Shringi tirtha* tank; ASI-protected |
| **Kolaramma Temple** | Kolar | Ganga origin, **Chola** rebuild (11th c) | Goddess temple with Chola inscriptions; scorpion deity (Chelamma) shrine |
| **Ramalingeshwara group** | **Avani**, Kolar | Nolamba/Chola, 10th c | Group of Shiva shrines; Avani is linked to Valmiki legend |
| **Kalleshwara Temple** | **Aralaguppe**, Tumakuru | Nolamba, 9th–10th c | Famous ceiling panel of dancing Shiva surrounded by *dikpalas* |
| **Ranganatha Temple** | **Srirangapatna**, Mandya | **894 CE** (Ganga, Tirumalaiah); expanded by Hoysalas, Vijayanagara and Wodeyars | "**Adi Ranga**", first of the three Ranganatha shrines on the Kaveri (Madhya Ranga at Shivanasamudra, Antya Ranga at Srirangam, Tamil Nadu) |

## 5. Badami Chalukyas (543–757 CE)

**Political history:** Founded by **Pulakeshin I**, who fortified **Vatapi (Badami)** and performed the Ashwamedha. **Mangalesha** finished the cave temples. **Pulakeshin II** (610–642) defeated **Harshavardhana on the Narmada** (c. 618–619), as recorded in the **Aihole inscription (634 CE)** composed by court poet **Ravikirti** in the Meguti temple. Pulakeshin II was later killed when the Pallava **Narasimhavarman I ("Vatapikonda")** captured Badami (642). **Vikramaditya I** recovered the kingdom. **Vikramaditya II** captured **Kanchi** three times. His queens **Lokamahadevi** and **Trailokyamahadevi** built the Virupaksha and Mallikarjuna temples at Pattadakal. **Kirtivarman II** was overthrown by the Rashtrakuta **Dantidurga** (753).
Chinese pilgrim **Hiuen Tsang** visited Pulakeshin II's kingdom.

### 5B. Aihole, Bagalkot: "Cradle of Indian temple architecture"
About **125 temples** in groups (Kontigudi, Galaganatha, Ambigera, Tryambakeshwara, Ramalinga and others), and the first Chalukyan capital.

| Temple | Date | Details |
|---|---|---|

| **Durga Temple** | Late 7th–early 8th c | **Apsidal (*gajaprishta*, elephant-back) plan** modelled on Buddhist chaitya halls; name comes from *durg* (fort), **not** the goddess Durga; peristyle walkway |
| **Huchimalligudi** | 7th c | Earliest example of an **antarala** (vestibule) between sanctum and hall |

**"Pattada Kisuvolal"**, the **coronation site** of the Chalukyas, on the Malaprabha river. It has 10 major temples: **four Nagara, four Dravida, one mixed (Papanatha) and one Jain**.

| **Papanatha** | **Mixed Nagara + Dravida** | c. 680–740 | Ramayana friezes |
| **Galaganatha** | Nagara (curvilinear *rekha-prasada*) | 8th c | Sculpture of Shiva killing Andhakasura |
| **Kashi Vishweshwara** | Nagara | 8th c | **Last** of the early Chalukyan temples here |
| **Kadasiddheshwara**, **Jambulingeshwara** | Nagara | 7th c | Small early Nagara shrines |
| **Chandrashekhara** | Dravida (no tower) | 8th c | Small |

## 6. Rashtrakutas (753–973 CE)

**Political history:** Founded by **Dantidurga**. Capital at **Manyakheta (Malkhed, Kalaburagi)**. **Krishna I** built the **Kailasa temple at Ellora** (Maharashtra). **Dhruva** and **Govinda III** raided north India (the "tripartite struggle" for Kannauj). **Amoghavarsha I** was a Jain-leaning patron, compared to Ashoka by historians, and author/patron of **Kavirajamarga** (earliest extant Kannada work on poetics, by Srivijaya). Arab traveller **Sulaiman** ranked him among the four great kings of the world. **Krishna III** defeated the Cholas at Takkolam (949). The dynasty was overthrown by **Tailapa II** (Kalyani Chalukya) in 973.

| Temple | Place | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **Navalinga Temple** | **Kukkanur**, Koppal | 9th c; group of **nine shrines** with lingas, Dravida style |
| **Kalleshwara Temple** | Kukkanur | Rashtrakuta to early Kalyani Chalukya transition |
| **Jain Narayana** | Pattadakal | See 5C |
| **Neminatha Basadi** | Malkhed (Manyakheta) | Jain centre in the Rashtrakuta capital |
| **Sirval / Sannati area temples** | Kalaburagi | Rashtrakuta-era shrines on the Bhima river |

## 7. Kalyani / Western Chalukyas (973–1189 CE)

**Political history:** **Tailapa II** revived Chalukya power. Capital at **Kalyani (Basavakalyan, Bidar)**. **Vikramaditya VI** (1076–1126) founded the **Chalukya-Vikrama era** (1076). His court had **Bilhana** (*Vikramankadevacharita*) and **Vijnaneshwara** (*Mitakshara*, the commentary on Hindu inheritance law). The empire declined after the **Kalachuri Bijjala** usurped power. The **Vachana / Sharana movement of Basavanna** (12th c) took place at Kalyani: **Anubhava Mantapa**, Bijjala's court. Patronage passed to Hoysalas, Seunas (Yadavas) and Kakatiyas.
**Style:** Vesara; soapstone; dense decoration; known as the **"Gadag school"**, centred on Gadag, Dharwad, Haveri, Koppal and Ballari districts.

## 8. Hoysalas (c. 1006–1346 CE)

**Political history:** Hill chieftains from **Sosevur (Angadi, Chikkamagaluru)**. The emblem shows **Sala killing a tiger (lion)**. **Vinayaditya** and **Ereyanga** were Chalukyan feudatories. **Vishnuvardhana (Bittideva, c. 1108–1152)** defeated the **Cholas at Talakad (1116)** and was influenced by **Ramanujacharya** (Sri Vaishnavism); his queen **Shantala Devi** was a Jain and a celebrated dancer. Capital moved from **Belur** to **Dwarasamudra (Halebidu)**. **Veera Ballala II** declared independence (c. 1187) and defeated the Seunas. **Narasimha III** ruled in the north while **Ramanatha** ruled the Tamil country (division of the kingdom). **Malik Kafur** sacked Dwarasamudra in **1311**. **Veera Ballala III** was killed fighting the Madurai Sultanate (1343). The Hoysala lands passed to **Vijayanagara**.
About 1,500 temples were built; around **92–100 survive**.

### The three UNESCO temples (2023)

| Temple | Place | Date / patron | Key details |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chennakeshava Temple (Vijayanarayana)** | **Belur**, Hassan (Yagachi river) | **1117**, **Vishnuvardhana**, to celebrate the **Talakad victory over the Cholas** | Took about **103 years** to finish (three generations). Star-shaped *jagati*; **42 madanikas** (bracket figures) including **Darpana Sundari** (lady with mirror). **Narasimha pillar** (once said to rotate). **Kappe Chennigaraya** shrine (built by Queen Shantala). **Rayagopura** added by **Vijayanagara**. Legend of sculptor **Jakanachari** and his son **Dakanachari**. **Living temple**; Belur rathotsava. |
| **Hoysaleshwara Temple** | **Halebidu (Dwarasamudra)**, Hassan | **c. 1121**, built by **Ketamalla**, an officer of Vishnuvardhana | **Twin shrines**: Hoysaleshwara (the king) and **Shantaleshwara** (Queen Shantala), each with a **Nandi mantapa**. Over 240 wall images; **frieze of 2,000 elephants**. Sculptor **Kedaroja**. Left **unfinished**; damaged in the **1311 (Malik Kafur)** and later raids. **Dorasamudra lake**. Halebidu also has the **Jain basadis** (Parshvanatha, Shantinatha, Adinatha; Parshvanatha basadi 1133 by **Boppa**, son of general Gangaraja) and the **Kedareshwara temple** (1219, Veera Ballala II). ASI museum. |
| **Keshava Temple** | **Somanathapura**, Mysuru (Kaveri bank) | **1268**, **Somanatha Dandanayaka**, general of **Narasimha III** | **Trikuta** (three shrines: Keshava, Janardhana, Venugopala); 16-pointed star plan; 64 cells in the cloister. **Best-preserved complete Hoysala temple.** Sculptor **Ruvari Mallitamma** signed many images. No longer worshipped (ASI monument). |

## 9. Vijayanagara Empire (1336–1646 CE)

**Political history:** Founded in **1336** by **Harihara I and Bukka I** (Sangama dynasty), guided by **Vidyaranya** of Sringeri. The four dynasties were **Sangama, Saluva, Tuluva and Aravidu**. **Devaraya II** brought in Muslim cavalry and archers; **Abdur Razzaq** (Persian envoy) visited in his reign. **Krishnadevaraya** (Tuluva, **1509–1529**) won at **Raichur (1520)** and **Udayagiri (Odisha)**, wrote *Amuktamalyada* (Telugu), and kept the **Ashtadiggajas** poets at court. Foreign visitors included **Nicolo de Conti** (Italian), **Domingo Paes** and **Fernao Nuniz** (Portuguese). The empire was defeated at the **Battle of Talikota (Rakkasa-Tangadi), 1565**, by the combined Deccan Sultanates; Hampi was sacked for about six months. The Aravidu line moved to **Penukonda, Chandragiri and Vellore**. Successor states: the **Mysuru Wodeyars, Keladi Nayakas, Madurai, Tanjore and Gingee Nayakas**.

## 13. Other monuments: Buddhist, prehistoric, Indo-Islamic, colonial

### 13A. Prehistoric and Mauryan
- **Hirebenakal** (Koppal): the **largest megalithic necropolis** in south India (about 2,000 burials, dolmens), roughly 800 BCE–200 BCE. Proposed for UNESCO (2026).
- **Maski** (Raichur): **Ashokan minor rock edict** discovered by **C. Beadon (1915)**. It is the **first edict to name "Devanampiya Asoka"** directly. - **Brahmagiri** (Chitradurga): excavated by **Mortimer Wheeler (1947)**; Neolithic–megalithic sequence.
- **Sanganakallu** and **Kupgal** (Ballari): Neolithic sites, rock art.

## 15. One-line quick-revision facts

- **Oldest Kannada inscription:** Halmidi (c. 450 CE, Kadamba).
- **Oldest dated structural temple in Karnataka:** Meguti, Aihole (634 CE).
- **"Cradle of Indian temple architecture":** Aihole.
- **Coronation site of the Chalukyas:** Pattadakal.
- **Apsidal (elephant-back) temple:** Durga temple, Aihole.
- **18-armed Nataraja:** Badami Cave 1. **Largest cave, inscription of 578 CE:** Cave 3 (Mangalesha).
- **Virupaksha, Pattadakal:** Lokamahadevi, after Vikramaditya II's win over the Pallavas; model for Ellora's Kailasa.
- **"Devalaya Chakravarti":** Mahadeva temple, Itagi (1112).
- **Attimabbe "Danachintamani":** Brahma Jinalaya, Lakkundi (1007).
- **Belur (1117):** Vishnuvardhana, after the Talakad victory over the Cholas. **Halebidu (1121):** Ketamalla. **Somanathapura (1268):** Somanatha Dandanayaka.
- **Earliest Hoysala temple:** Lakshmidevi, Doddagaddavalli (1113).
- **Hoysala UNESCO listing:** Sept 2023, India's **42nd** WHS.
- **Stone chariot on the ₹50 note:** Vittala temple, Hampi.
- **Musical pillars:** Vittala temple, Hampi.
- **Battle of Talikota / Rakkasa-Tangadi:** 1565.
- **Vidyashankara, Sringeri (1338):** 12 zodiac pillars.
- **Gommateshwara:** 57 ft, Chavundaraya, c. 981–983; next Mahamastakabhisheka in 2030.
- **Thousand-pillar basadi:** Mudabidri (1430).
- **Gavi Gangadhareshwara:** sunlight on the linga at Makara Sankranti.
- **First mention of "Bengaluru":** Begur inscription (c. 890 CE).
- **Kanakana Kindi:** Udupi Krishna Matha.
- **Atmalinga:** Gokarna (and the Murudeshwar legend).
- **Richest Muzrai temple:** Kukke Subramanya (₹167.89 crore, 2025–26).
- **Temple open once a year:** Hasanamba, Hassan.
- **Chamundeshwari gopura:** Krishnaraja Wodeyar III (1827); **Nandi** on Chamundi Hills: 1659.
- **"Hakim Nanjunda":** Tipu Sultan's name for Nanjundeshwara, Nanjangud.
- **Ashoka portrait labelled "Raya Asoka":** Kanaganahalli (Sannati).
- **Maski edict:** first to name "Asoka" (found 1915).
- **Gol Gumbaz:** 1656, Muhammad Adil Shah; whispering gallery.
- **Jama Masjid, Gulbarga:** 1367, no courtyard.
- **2026 news hooks:** Neeragunda hero stone (Vishnuvardhana's death, 11 Dec 1141); Lakkundi excavation (3,000+ artefacts); Shravanabelagola, Lakkundi and Hirebenakal UNESCO proposal; HC quashing the takeover of Gali Anjaneya temple; Hasanamba jatra; Dasara 11–21 Oct; TTD temple land at Surathkal; Hampi excavation (drainage system, Jain temple roof, Alvar idols).
